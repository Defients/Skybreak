# Skyward Ascent Canon Bible

THIS DOCUMENT IS THE CANONICAL SOURCE OF TRUTH FOR SKYWARD ASCENT.

If existing project content conflicts with this document, the Canon Bible takes precedence unless a contradiction would break gameplay. Preserve gameplay whenever possible while updating names, lore, terminology, flavor, art direction, and worldbuilding to align with the canon.

Do not invent replacement lore that contradicts this document. When uncertain, preserve mystery rather than fabricate new canon.

## An Astrizda Game

## 0. Core Identity

**Skyward Ascent** is a tabletop card-and-dice dungeon crawl set within **Astrizda**.

Players use a standard 54-card deck and two dice to assemble a party of three Heroes and climb the **Astrilith**, an ancient vertical reality-structure whose floors intersect forgotten regions, broken timelines, guarded vaults, and living echoes of Astrizda’s history.

The game is designed to be fully playable as a standalone fantasy adventure. New players do **not** need to know Astrizda lore to understand or enjoy it.

The deeper truth is revealed gradually:

> The climb is not merely a dungeon crawl.  
> It is a test of balance, meaning, will, and resonance.

# 3. Canon Status

## Canon Tier

Skyward Ascent should be treated as:

> **Playable Canon / Mythic Trial Canon**

That means events, locations, artifacts, and characters are real within Astrizda, but the repeated runs are interpreted as ritualized, probabilistic, or resonance-based retellings of the climb.

A run is not necessarily “one literal historical event.”

Instead, each run represents:

- a possible ascent
    
- a remembered ascent
    
- a simulated ascent
    
- a resonance echo
    
- a trial generated by the Astrilith
    
- a mythic route toward Vyridian
    

This protects gameplay randomness while preserving lore integrity.

## Canon Rule

Every run can happen.

Not every run happened the same way.

# 4. The Central Setting

## The Astrilith

The Spire is now canonically replaced by:

> **The Astrilith**

The Astrilith is an ancient vertical construct within Astrizda.

It is not merely a tower.

It is:

- a reality-anchor
    
- a harmonic structure
    
- a vertical world
    
- a trial engine
    
- a memory-vault
    
- a threshold between planes
    
- a place where meaning, power, and existence overlap
    

The Astrilith intersects with multiple regions of Astrizda. Each floor may contain ruins, forests, observatories, vaults, sanctuaries, battlefields, laboratories, shrines, dream-chambers, and impossible stairways.

## Public Description

Use this version early:

> The Astrilith is an ancient tower said to rise through forgotten skies, sealed vaults, ruined kingdoms, and celestial thresholds. Those who climb it are changed by what they survive.

## Internal Truth

The Astrilith is tied to balance, resonance, causality, and the Astril Conductor role. Vyridian is bound to it as guardian, conductor, and final arbiter.

Do not explain all of that on the homepage.

# 5. The Final Confrontation

## Replace Apexus

The old final boss name:

> Apexus, the Astral Overlord

should be retired or treated as outdated placeholder lore.

Reasons:

- “Astral” conflicts with the established **Astril** language.
    
- “Overlord” reads generic villain.
    
- Apexus is less connected to existing Astrizda canon.
    
- The first game should strengthen the real mythos.
    

## Canon Final Figure

The summit figure is:

> **Vyridian, the Astril Conductor**

Correct spelling: **Vyridian**.

Vyridian is not a simple villain.

Vyridian is:

- guardian of the Astrilith
    
- cosmic harmonic conductor
    
- balance absolutist
    
- final arbiter
    
- regulator of structure
    
- counterbalance to unchecked Convergence
    
- connected to Ymzo, Kiox, the S.Crystal, and Fyxius/Fixius
    
- feared, respected, and misunderstood
    

## The Final Encounter

The player does not simply “kill Vyridian.”

The final encounter should be framed as:

- a confrontation
    
- a trial
    
- a resonance test
    
- a harmonic judgment
    
- a battle of worthiness
    
- a balancing ordeal
    

Victory means the party survives the judgment and completes the ascent.

Better phrasing:

> Face Vyridian at the summit.

Avoid:

> Defeat Vyridian and claim the tower.

## Public-Facing Version

> Ascend toward the summit, where Vyridian, the Astril Conductor, awaits.

## Deeper Version

> Vyridian does not guard treasure. Vyridian guards imbalance from becoming law.

# 6. The Player Fantasy

The player fantasy is not:

> I beat a tower.

The player fantasy is:

> I assembled a party, entered an impossible structure, survived mythic dangers, and proved worthy before a force older than kingdoms.

The player should feel:

- wonder
    
- danger
    
- cleverness
    
- discovery
    
- progression
    
- mystery
    
- triumph
    
- consequence
    

The climb should always feel upward.

Even when the player descends into caves, vaults, or memories, the emotional direction remains:

> ascent.

Skyward Ascent should reveal Astrizda in layers.

### Layer 1 — Instantly Clear

Players understand:

- climb the Astrilith
    
- build a party
    
- fight enemies
    
- collect loot
    
- reach the summit
    
- face Vyridian
    

### Layer 2 — Curious Flavor

Players begin noticing:

- recurring symbols
    
- faction hints
    
- mysterious room text
    
- names like Ymzo, Kiox, Sinira, VyCorp, HYRUM
    
- references to resonance, fracture, balance, and echoes
    

### Layer 3 — Deep Lore

Dedicated players discover:

- Zya / Zaya / Kyn
    
- Astril Continuum legacy
    
- AHYBE
    
- S.Crystal
    
- Convergence
    
- VyCorp world-lock logic
    
- Vyridian’s true role
    
- Ymzo and Kiox’s philosophical conflict
    
- Sinira’s wound-logic
    
- Nit as termination principle
    

Layer 3 should be optional.

Never force it onto first-time players.

# 10. Game Structure

## Core Loop

1. Assemble a party of three Heroes.
    
2. Enter the Astrilith.
    
3. Draw cards to reveal rooms, enemies, events, loot, and trials.
    
4. Resolve encounters using cards, dice, Hero abilities, and choices.
    
5. Gain loot and progression.
    
6. Climb floor by floor.
    
7. Reach the summit.
    
8. Face Vyridian’s judgment.
    
9. Complete, fail, or transform the ascent.
    

## Run Identity

Each run should feel like a mythic attempt.

Suggested terms:

- Run
    
- Ascent
    
- Climb
    
- Trial
    
- Route
    
- Echo
    

Best public term:

> Run

Best flavorful term:

> Ascent

Use both:

> Start New Run  
> Begin a New Ascent

# 11. Hero Canon

## Initial Hero Design Philosophy

The first Heroes should be understandable archetypes with Astrizda flavor.

Do not immediately make every Hero require deep lore knowledge.

A good pattern:

> simple role + specific Astrizda identity

Example:

- The Warden
    
- The Astrimancer
    
- The Riftblade
    
- The Echo-Seer
    
- The Relicbound
    
- The Wayfarer
    

Later unlocks can reveal major canon figures.

## Hero Tiers

### Tier 1 — Starter Heroes

These introduce mechanics.

They should be approachable.

Example roles:

- Warden — defense / protection
    
- Astrimancer — magic / manipulation
    
- Wayfarer — mobility / scouting
    
- Relicblade — offense / equipment synergy
    
- Echo-Seer — card manipulation / prediction
    
- Sanctum Cleric — healing / stabilization
    

### Tier 2 — Lore Heroes

These carry stronger Astrizda identity.

Example:

- Luthirion Adept
    
- Loturian Exile
    
- VyCorp Defector
    
- HYRUM Scout
    
- Crystal-Bound Pilgrim
    
- Astrilith Keeper
    

### Tier 3 — Canon Unlocks

These are major characters.

Use with care.

Possible unlockable or expansion characters:

- Ymzo
    
- Kiox
    
- Sinira
    
- Nymira
    
- Thajal
    
- Vyridian-adjacent avatars or echoes
    
- Shiz

# 12. Factions and Enemy Logic

Enemies should not feel random.

- ancient
    
- lawful

- Riftmaw
    
- Shard-Hound
    
- Hollowwing
    
- Glassbone Stalker

# 18. Vyridian Encounter Design

## Final Encounter Tone

The final confrontation should feel unlike every other battle.

It should be:

- quiet at first
    
- ceremonial
    
- cosmic
    
- judgmental
    
- beautiful
    
- terrifying
    
- balanced
    

## Vyridian Should Test

- party composition
    
- accumulated corruption / instability
    
- reliance on extremes
    
- use of cursed items
    
- balance between offense, defense, healing, and utility
    
- whether the player exploited too much power without paying costs
    

## Possible Final Phases

### Phase 1 — The Measure

Vyridian evaluates the party.

Mechanic:

- checks current state
    
- applies judgment modifiers
    
- calls back prior choices
    

### Phase 2 — The Conduction

Vyridian manipulates the rules.

Mechanic:

- suit effects change
    
- dice doubles matter
    
- cards may invert
    

### Phase 3 — The Harmonic Trial

The party must survive and stabilize.

Mechanic:

- not just damage race
    
- balance-based victory condition
    
- player must manage risk carefully
    

### Phase 4 — The Verdict

Possible outcomes:

- Ascent Completed
    
- Harmony Achieved
    
- Judgment Survived
    
- Summit Rejected
    
- Conductor Replaced, secret ending
    
- Echo Returned, loop ending
    

## Win Language

Use:

- Survived the Judgment
    
- Completed the Ascent
    
- Reached the Summit
    
- Achieved Harmonic Balance
    

Avoid:

- Killed Vyridian
    
- Destroyed the Conductor
    
- Conquered the Astrilith

# 19. Endings

Skyward Ascent should eventually support multiple endings.

## Standard Victory

> The party survives Vyridian’s judgment and completes the ascent.

## Perfect Balance Ending

> The party reaches the summit with minimal corruption, balanced choices, and stable resonance.

## Power-Cost Ending

> The party wins, but becomes marked by what they used.

## Rejection Ending

> Vyridian denies the ascent. The party survives, but the summit remains sealed.

## Loop Ending

> The climb repeats. Something was missed.

## Conductor Ending

Rare.

> One Hero accepts the burden of balance and is changed forever.

This ending should be rare and heavy.

- high fantasy
    
- celestial mythology
    
- vertical architecture
    
- floating ruins
    
- impossible stairways
    
- ancient marble
    
- gold inlays
    
- sapphire shadows

- volumetric light
    
- floating islands
    
- vertical scale
    
- ancient stone

- neon overload
    
- random sci-fi panels everywhere

- white stone / cold marble

- suspended platforms
    
- ring-like harmonic structures
    
- floating stairways

- reality fractures
    
- blue-white light
    
- violet storm near the summit
    

A viewer should eventually recognize it without text.

# 24. Canon Mystery Rules

## Explain Less Than You Know

Every piece of lore should imply more than it states.

Good:

> “The Conductor has measured you before.”

Bad:

> “Vyridian has judged your previous timeline because the Astrilith stores all possible resonance states.”

The first one creates curiosity.

The second one kills mystery.

## Use Names as Hooks

Names like Ymzo, Kiox, Sinira, HYRUM, and VyCorp should appear first as intriguing fragments.

Example:

> A cracked note bears Ymzo’s seal.

That is better than a full biography.

# 26. Continuity Laws

These should not be violated.

## Law 1 — Astril, Not Astral

Use **Astril** for canon concepts.

Avoid “Astral” unless intentionally representing outsider misunderstanding.

## Law 2 — Vyridian Is Not Evil

Vyridian can be terrifying, severe, and dangerous.

But not cartoonishly evil.

## Law 3 — The Astrilith Is Not Just A Tower

It is a reality-scale harmonic structure.

## Law 4 — Mystery Beats Explanation

First-time players get wonder.

Lore-hunters get depth.

## Law 5 — Gameplay Can Be Mythic Echo

Repeated randomized runs do not break canon because each run can be a resonance echo, trial, possible ascent, or mythic retelling.

## Law 6 — Generic Fantasy Must Be Astrizda-Flavored

A sword is never just a sword if it can be a Warden’s Blade.

A treasure room is never just a treasure room if it can be a Relic Vault.

## Law 7 — The Game Must Stand Alone

A player who knows nothing about Astrizda should still understand the game.
